`law` `justice` `politics`

Bail is a fundamental part of Australia's criminal justice system, based on the principle that a person is presumed innocent until proven guilty. Under the Bail Act 2013 (NSW) , courts must determine whether an accused person poses an unacceptable risk of failing to appear in court, committing further offences, endangering others, or interfering with the administration of justice. Bail decisions therefore require a careful balance between protecting the rights of the accused and ensuring community safety. Recent high-profile domestic violence and youth crime cases have intensified public debate about whether that balance is being achieved.
